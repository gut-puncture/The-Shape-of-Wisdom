\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We followed the state from which four language models answer short factual questions, layer by layer. In each model, the answer becomes readable within a single layer, and from that onset on, questions with the same answer gather around a shared centroid that does not depend on how the question is asked. Before the onset the answer is partly readable but cannot be steered; from the onset on, moving a state to a centroid makes the model give that centroid's answer. Wrong answers sit near centroids too, and at the onset a near miss already shows the right answer as a close runner-up. Whether the same picture holds for open-ended answers, for reasoning over several steps and for larger models is the natural next question.
