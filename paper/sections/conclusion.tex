\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We recorded the state from which four language models answer short factual questions and followed it through every layer. In all four, the answer the model will give becomes readable from the state within a single layer, at a depth that differs between models but is fixed within each. From that depth on, questions with the same answer gather in a shared region, reached alike from different relations and wordings and readable without the directions that write the answer's name. Moving a state into a region after that depth makes the model give the region's answer, and moving it before that depth does nothing. Regions follow the answer the model gives rather than the true one, so a wrong answer sits somewhere specific, and where it sits predicts whether the model still recognises the right answer better than its confidence does. Whether the same picture holds for open-ended answers, for reasoning over several steps and for larger models is the natural next question.
