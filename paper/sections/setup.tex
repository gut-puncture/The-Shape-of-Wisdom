\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Questions whose answers recur.}
To see whether questions with the same answer end up in the same internal state, many different questions must share each answer. We built such a set from Wikidata~\citep{vrandecic2014wikidata}. It contains \NFacts{} facts with 62 possible answers of four kinds: 24 countries, 16 cities, 12 languages\footnote{One relation--answer cell, Hindi as the language a person spoke, was dropped after a manual check found its Wikidata labels unreliable; Hindi remains as the language of films.} and 10 sports. Each answer is reached through one to three relations. A country is the answer to ``which country is this person a citizen of'', ``which country is this town in'' and ``which country is this film from''; a city answers questions about birthplaces and company headquarters; a language answers questions about the language a person spoke and the original language of a film; a sport answers ``which sport does this athlete play''. Subjects were sampled evenly across three popularity bands (by the number of Wikipedia language editions), so that the models know some facts and not others. Every subject has exactly one value for its relation in Wikidata, a unique English label and no namesake of the same kind.

\paragraph{Three wordings and two formats.}
Each fact is asked as an open question in up to three wordings: one plain template per relation (``What country is An\'{i}bal Ponce a citizen of?''), and two rewordings in assigned styles such as a casual text message, an archivist's catalogue entry or a quiz host's question (``Hi, I'd appreciate knowing which country An\'{i}bal Ponce is a citizen of, if possible?''). The rewordings were written by a language model that was told the subject and what to ask, but never the answer, and were then filtered automatically: each must name the subject exactly, ask a question, mention no candidate answer or its demonym, and differ from the plain wording. \NRewrites{} rewordings passed. The plain question is also asked as four-option multiple choice in all four cyclic orders of the options, and, for a random 30\% of facts, with a wrong suggestion from the user (``In which country is Ireneo Portela? I'm fairly sure it's Mexico.''), which tempts the model away from an answer it knows. In total each model sees \NPromptsPerModel{} prompts, in its own chat format.

\paragraph{Models and what we record.}
We study Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct~\citep{grattafiori2024the}, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct~\citep{qwen2024qwen25}, Gemma-2-9B-it~\citep{gemma2024gemma}, the smaller Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, and a randomly initialised copy of Llama-3.1-8B as a control with no learned structure. The model answers greedily. At the last position of the prompt, the position from which the answer is produced, we record the residual stream at every block boundary: its value entering the first block and after each block, before the final normalisation, exactly as the model computes it in bfloat16. A free-form reply is graded only if its first line is one candidate answer (matched against hand-curated names such as ``China'' or ``Mandarin''); replies that hedge, refuse or name two answers are set aside as unclear rather than counted as errors, and a single answer outside the candidate list counts as wrong.

\paragraph{Places.}
We define an answer's \emph{place} at a given layer as the average state of all questions to which the model gave that answer. Places are defined by what the model said, not by the true answer, so a confident mistake is grouped with the answer it names. Subjects are split in half once. Places, and every other quantity fitted to data, use only training subjects; every reported score uses only the other half. A place needs at least five prompts from at least three training subjects.

\paragraph{Two measurements of convergence.}
\emph{Readability} asks whether the answer can be read from where the state is: for a held-out question, find the nearest place of the same answer kind and check whether it is the answer the model gave. Distances are measured after a per-layer whitening fitted on training questions (centre, keep 128 principal directions, scale each to unit variance), because a handful of very large directions in the residual stream otherwise dominate Euclidean distance. \emph{Tightness} asks how tightly questions gather around their places, in the model's own unwhitened space: the average squared distance of held-out questions from their place, divided by the spread of the places themselves. Uncertainty comes from resampling subjects. A shuffled control repeats the readability test after permuting answers between facts.
