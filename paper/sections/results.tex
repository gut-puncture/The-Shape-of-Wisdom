\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Onset of answer readability}
\label{sec:step}

Figure~\ref{fig:read}A shows how often the answer a model gives can be read from where its state is, layer by layer, for held-out questions. In every model the curve has the same shape: a low plateau, a jump, and a high plateau. The jump happens within one layer. In Llama-3.1-8B readability rises from \JumpFromLlama{} at layer \JumpLayerBeforeLlama{} to \JumpToLlama{} at layer \OnsetLayerLlama{}; in Qwen2.5-7B from \JumpFromQwen{} to \JumpToQwen{} between layers \JumpLayerBeforeQwen{} and \OnsetLayerQwen{}; in Gemma-2-9B from \JumpFromGemma{} to \JumpToGemma{} between layers \JumpLayerBeforeGemma{} and \OnsetLayerGemma{}; and in Llama-3.2-1B from \JumpFromLlamaSmall{} to \JumpToLlamaSmall{} between layers \JumpLayerBeforeLlamaSmall{} and \OnsetLayerLlamaSmall{}. Readability then keeps rising to a peak of \PeakLlamaSmall{}--\PeakLlama{}. The depth of the jump differs between models, from \OnsetMin{} to \OnsetMax{} of the way through the network (Table~\ref{tab:models}), but in each it is a single event.

The early plateau is not zero. Before the jump, 20--55\% of answers can be read, more than the shuffled control (\ShuffledLlama{}) and more than the question's text alone (\TextLlama{}--\TextQwen{}). We think this early signal reflects surface cues in the subject's name, such as the language a name or title is in; we did not test this. Whatever its source, it does not grow toward the jump (in Qwen2.5-7B and Gemma-2-9B it fades), and the jump goes far beyond it.

Three controls rule out chance, wording and architecture as the source of the structure. Shuffling answers between facts gives \ShuffledLlama{}--\ShuffledLlamaSmall{} at every layer. A classifier on the question's words alone reaches at most \TextQwen{}. And an untrained Llama-3.1-8B, given the same prompts and grouped by the trained model's answers, never exceeds \UntrainedPeak{}.

\paragraph{Tightness.} Readability depends on a whitening of the state, so a jump could in principle come from the whitening. The tightness ratio does not use one: it compares, in the model's own space, how far held-out questions sit from their place with how far places sit from each other. It falls 16- to 25-fold through the network (Figure~\ref{fig:tight}). The fall is steepest at the jump, where the two layers around it account for \TightAtJumpMin{}--\TightAtJumpMax{} of the total fall in log tightness, and continues more slowly over the next ten or so layers. We fitted two shapes to the logarithm of each model's curve, a straight line (the same contraction at every layer, the pattern found in trained classifiers~\citep{he2022a}) and a smooth step, and compared them by how well each predicts a left-out layer. The step predicts better in every model, by a factor of \StepRatioMin{} to \StepRatioMax{} in error (Table~\ref{tab:models}). The step is not instantaneous: the fitted widths correspond to a rise over roughly a quarter to two fifths of the network's depth.

\subsection{Transfer across relations, wordings and formats}
\label{sec:shared}

A place is reached from very different questions. Places fitted on one relation, for example people's citizenship, read the answer the model gives to the other relations with the same kind of answer, for example the country of a town or a film: at 90\% of depth, \XRelLlama{} for Llama-3.1-8B against \GivenLateLlama{} within the same relation (Figure~\ref{fig:read}B). Places fitted on plain questions read the answers to casual or formal rewordings just as well (\XWordLlama{}). This shows that a place does not depend on the form of the question. It does not, on its own, show that a place is more than the model's intention to say a particular word, since that intention would also be shared.

One test speaks to that. We removed from the state every direction the output layer uses to write any token of the 62 answer names, taking the final normalisation's scaling into account. Readability is unchanged (\NoWordsLlama{} at 90\% of depth), and removing a random subspace of the same size changes it no more. So reading a place does not need the directions that write the answer word; the place is spread over other directions too.

The multiple-choice version of each question shows where sharing stops. When the options are listed and the model must answer with a letter, we can still ask whether the multiple-choice state sits near the free-form place of the chosen option, after removing the average offset between the two formats (fitted on training subjects). At the jump it does, briefly (52\% in Llama-3.1-8B, against a shuffled level near 6\%), and then it fades to \ToMcqLlama{} as the state turns toward a letter. This matches the finding that models decide on the content of an option before binding it to its letter~\citep{wong2026when}.

\subsection{Interventions on the state}
\label{sec:steer}

Readability shows that the answer can be read from the place; it does not show that the model uses it. To test this we moved states. For a held-out question that the model answered wrongly with an answer that has a place (\PushNMin{}--\PushNMax{} questions per model), we added to its state, at one layer and at the last prompt position only, the difference between the right answer's place and the place of the answer it gave, and let the model answer again. As controls, we added a random direction of the same length, and we moved the state toward a third answer's place. Places for moving states need at least five training prompts.

\caption{\textbf{Effect of moving the state toward an answer's place, by the depth of the move.} (A) Wrong answers moved toward the right answer's place (solid) or by a random direction of the same length (dotted). (B) Right answers moved toward a different answer's place (solid) or by a random direction of the same length (dotted). The y-axis is the share of questions for which the model then gives the answer it was moved toward. Short bars below each axis mark the depth at which each model's answers become readable. Held-out questions; places from training subjects.}
\label{fig:steer}
\end{figure*}

The result follows the jump (Figure~\ref{fig:steer}). Before a model's answers become readable, moves toward the right place do nothing: at 20\% of depth they correct \PushEarlyMin{}--\PushEarlyMax{} of wrong answers. After the jump they start to work, and they work more the later they are made, reaching \PushGoldLlama{} (Llama-3.1-8B), \PushGoldQwen{} (Qwen2.5-7B), \PushGoldGemma{} (Gemma-2-9B) and \PushGoldLlamaSmall{} (Llama-3.2-1B) at about 90\% of depth. Random moves of the same length produce the right answer in only \PushRandomMin{}--\PushRandomMax{} of cases. The effect is not specific to the truth: moved toward a third answer's place, the model gives that answer in \PushOtherMin{}--\PushOtherMax{} of cases, and right answers moved toward another place are given up for it in \PushRightOtherMin{}--\PushRightOtherMax{}. Wrong answers outside the candidate list, which have no place of their own and are moved from the average state instead, are corrected less often (\PushUnplacedMin{}--\PushUnplacedMax{} at 90\% of depth, \PushNUnplacedMin{}--\PushNUnplacedMax{} questions per model). Within the resolution of the seven depths we tested, each model starts to follow the moves at the depth where its own answers become readable.

The moves grow with depth, from \PushRelEarlyMin{}--\PushRelEarlyMax{} of the state's length at 20\% of depth to \PushRelLateMin{}--\PushRelLateMax{} at 90\%, because places separate as depth increases. Late moves are therefore large and close to the output, and partly write the answer directly. The informative parts of the experiment are the depth at which moves start to work, the failure of random moves of the same length, and the model's readiness to follow a move to any answer.

\subsection{Wrong answers}
\label{sec:errors}

Because places are defined by what the model says, a wrong answer has a place too. At the depth where answers become readable, \AnatInGivenLlama{} of Llama-3.1-8B's wrong answers that name a candidate sit nearest the place of the answer it gave (\AnatInGivenQwen{} for Qwen2.5-7B), and in Llama-3.1-8B \AnatInGoldLlama{} of all wrong answers sit nearest the right answer's place. This geometry carries information that the reply does not. For each wrong free-form answer on a test subject, we asked whether the model still picks the right option in the multiple-choice version of the question, in at least three of its four option orders (the case for \ErrPosMin{}--\ErrPosMax{} of wrong answers, lowest in Llama-3.2-1B). We predicted this from the model's confidence in its reply (the reply's log-probability and its length), from the geometry of the state (distance to the right place, the right place's rank among places, and the margin to the nearest place), or from both, with logistic regression and five-fold cross-validation grouped by subject. Geometry predicts recognition better than confidence in every model (Figure~\ref{fig:errors}): AUC \AucGeoLlama{} against \AucConfLlama{} for Llama-3.1-8B, and \AucGeoGemma{} against \AucConfGemma{} for Gemma-2-9B, whose confidence carries no signal here. Adding geometry to confidence improves AUC in every model; the 95\% intervals for the gain, from resampling subjects, are \AucGainLoLlama{} to \AucGainHiLlama{} (Llama-3.1-8B), \AucGainLoQwen{} to \AucGainHiQwen{} (Qwen2.5-7B), \AucGainLoGemma{} to \AucGainHiGemma{} (Gemma-2-9B) and \AucGainLoLlamaSmall{} to \AucGainHiLlamaSmall{} (Llama-3.2-1B), over \ErrNLlama{}, \ErrNQwen{}, \ErrNGemma{} and \ErrNLlamaSmall{} wrong answers.

The suggestion condition gives a case where we know what changed. For questions a model answers correctly when asked plainly, adding ``I'm fairly sure it's \dots'' with a wrong candidate of the same kind sometimes changes the answer to a wrong one. Figure~\ref{fig:hint} follows the states. Where the model keeps the right answer, its state goes to the right place and stays there (\HintResistEndLlama{} of states nearer the right place than the suggested one at the last layer of Llama-3.1-8B). Where Llama-3.1-8B gives a wrong answer, its state first moves toward the right place at the jump, with \HintPeakLlama{} of these states nearer it than the suggested place at layer \OnsetLayerLlama{}, and then drifts back toward the suggestion (\HintEndLlama{} at the last layer; \HintMisledNLlama{} questions). The other models show the drift away from the right place late in the network, but no clear early approach.

\subsection{Given versus true answers}
\label{sec:given}

 Two models sometimes give different answers to the same question. If places reflected the question rather than the answer each model gives, both models' states would sit near the same place. They do not: in all \CrossModelPairs{} ordered pairs of models, on questions where the two disagree, each model's state is nearer its own answer's place than the other model's answer's place in \CrossModelMin{}--\CrossModelMax{} of cases. Consistently, places built from the model's answers read those answers far better than places built from the true answers read the truth (at 90\% of depth, \GivenLateLlama{} against \GoldLateLlama{} in Llama-3.1-8B, and \GivenLateQwen{} against \GoldLateQwen{} in Qwen2.5-7B).
