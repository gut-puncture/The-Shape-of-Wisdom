When a language model answers a factual question, where does its internal state go? We asked four open models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B and Llama-3.2-1B) \NFacts{} questions whose answers recur, and recorded the state from which each answer is produced at every layer. Questions with the same answer converge on a shared region, the answer's \emph{place}. They do not drift there gradually: in every model the answer becomes readable within a few layers at a fixed depth, between \OnsetLlama{} and \OnsetQwen{} of the way through the network, and a single step describes this far better than steady convergence. Places are concepts rather than words. A place learned from people's citizenships finds the country of a film or a town, survives the removal of the answer words' output directions, and is absent in an untrained network. Places are organised by what the model believes, not by what is true, and they are causal: after the step, moving a wrong answer's state into the right place makes the model give the right answer up to \PushGoldGemma{} of the time, while a random move of the same length works \PushRandomLlama{}--\PushRandomGemma{} of the time and moves before the step do nothing. Finally, where a wrong answer sits predicts whether the model still recognises the right one in multiple choice better than the model's own confidence does, in all four models.
