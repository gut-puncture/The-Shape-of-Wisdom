When a language model answers a factual question, where does its internal state go? We asked four open models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B and Llama-3.2-1B) \NFacts{} factual questions whose answers recur, such as the country a person is a citizen of. At every layer, we recorded the state from which the answer is produced. Questions that receive the same answer converge on a shared region of the space of states, which we call the answer's \emph{place}. The answer becomes readable from the state suddenly: in every model, readability jumps within a single layer, at a depth between \OnsetMin{} and \OnsetMax{} of the way through the network. A place does not depend on how the question is asked. For example, the place of a country learned from questions about people's citizenship also identifies that country when the question is about a town or a film. Places are also used by the model. After the jump, moving the state of a wrongly answered question into the right answer's place makes the model give the right answer in \PushGoldMin{}--\PushGoldMax{} of cases late in the network, against \PushRandomMin{}--\PushRandomMax{} for a random move of the same size. Before the jump, such moves do nothing. Finally, places reveal which mistakes are near misses. When a model answers wrongly but the right answer's place is close behind the place its state reached, the model usually picks the right answer when the question is asked as multiple choice. This geometry predicts such near misses better than the model's confidence does, in all four models.
