When a language model answers a factual question, where does its internal state go? We asked four open models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B and Llama-3.2-1B) \NFacts{} questions whose answers recur, and recorded the state from which each answer is produced at every layer. Questions to which a model gives the same answer converge on a shared region, the answer's \emph{place}. The answer does not become readable from the state gradually: in every model it jumps within a single layer, at a model-specific depth between \OnsetMin{} and \OnsetMax{} of the way through the network (most sharply when the answer is a name rather than a sport), and a step predicts the tightening of questions around their places far better than a constant per-layer contraction. Places are shared across questions: a place learned from people's citizenships reads the country the model gives for a film or a town, reading a place does not need the output directions of the answer words, and no such structure exists in an untrained network. Places are causal. After the jump, moving a wrong answer's state into the right answer's place makes the model give the right answer in \PushGoldMin{}--\PushGoldMax{} of cases at late layers, against \PushRandomMin{}--\PushRandomMax{} for a random move of the same length, and moves made before the jump do nothing. Finally, among wrong answers, where the state sits predicts whether the model still picks the right option in multiple choice better than the model's confidence in its reply does, in all four models.
