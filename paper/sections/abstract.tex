When a language model answers a factual question, where does its internal state go? We asked four open models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B and Llama-3.2-1B) \NFacts{} factual questions whose answers recur, such as the country a person is a citizen of. At every layer, we recorded the state from which the answer is produced. Questions that receive the same answer converge on a shared region of state space, which we summarise by its centre, the \emph{answer centroid}. Our findings are four. First, the answer becomes readable in a single layer. In every model, the share of questions whose state lies nearest its own answer's centroid jumps within one layer, at a depth between \OnsetMin{} and \OnsetMax{} of the way through the network. Second, centroids follow the model's answer, not the question: the centroid of a country learned from questions about people's citizenship also identifies that country in questions about towns and films. Third, the model uses the centroids. After the jump, moving the state of a wrongly answered question to the right answer's centroid makes the model give the right answer in \PushGoldMin{}--\PushGoldMax{} of cases late in the network. A random move of the same size does so in \PushRandomMin{}--\PushRandomMax{}. Before the jump, such moves do nothing, even when made as large as late ones (checked in one model). Fourth, centroids reveal near misses. When a model answers wrongly but the right answer's centroid is among the nearest to its state, the model usually picks the right answer in multiple choice. This signal separates near misses from real gaps in knowledge better than the model's confidence does, in all four models.
