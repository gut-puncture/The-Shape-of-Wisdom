\section{Details}
\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Questions.} Subjects were sampled from three popularity bands by number of Wikipedia language editions (1--4, 5--19, and 20 or more), roughly a third from each, because models know popular facts far better than rare ones~\citep{mallen2022when}. Every subject has one answer, a unique English name and no namesake of the same kind. We dropped Hindi as the language a person spoke after a manual check found its labels unreliable. A rewording was kept only if it named the subject exactly, asked a question, mentioned no candidate answer or its demonym, and differed from the plain question; \NRewrites{} passed. Multiple-choice questions show four options in all four rotations of their order. In total each model answers \NPromptsAnalysed{} prompts, each in its own chat format.

\paragraph{States and grading.} States are recorded before the final normalisation, exactly as the model computes them in bfloat16, at the last prompt position before the first layer and after every layer. A reply is graded by its first line, matched against hand-curated names for each answer (for example ``China'' or ``Mandarin''). A single answer outside the 62 candidates counts as wrong.

\paragraph{Centroids and readability.} A centroid is built only for an answer given to at least five training questions about at least three different subjects. Readability counts only test questions whose given answer has a centroid. The whitening keeps the 128 leading principal components of the training states at each layer and divides each by its standard deviation, after shrinking each variance 10\% toward the average variance so that directions with very little variance are not blown up. When a result is reported at a given depth, we use the nearest layer.

\paragraph{Onset layer.} We split the training subjects in half again, build centroids from one half, and measure readability on the other at every layer. The onset layer is the first layer at which this readability covers half the distance from chance to its highest value over layers, where chance is the average, over questions, of one over the number of candidate answers of the question's kind.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} Questions about one subject are not independent, so we resample subjects: we draw the test subjects with replacement 300 times, recompute the result each time, and report the middle 95\% of the results.

\paragraph{Controls.} Shuffled answers are permuted between facts of the same kind, all wordings of a fact keeping one answer; readability is averaged over five shuffles. For the question-text control, the TF-IDF vectors of character sequences of length two to four are reduced to 256 dimensions by a truncated singular value decomposition fitted on training questions.

\paragraph{Separation.} $D_{\text{between}}$ and $D_{\text{within}}$ are computed for each kind of answer separately and each is summed over kinds, weighting each kind by its number of test questions, before taking their ratio.

\paragraph{Centroids follow the answer.} For other relations, each test question's readability is averaged over the centroids built from each other relation of its kind. For answer words removed, each unembedding vector is first multiplied by the final normalisation's scale, the vectors are turned into an orthonormal basis, and each state is projected onto the space at right angles to that basis; the whitening is then refitted. The random control removes a random orthonormal basis of the same size. True-answer centroids are built like ordinary centroids but group training questions by their true answer. The cross-model comparison uses plain test questions at each model's onset layer.

\paragraph{Moves.} The seven depths are 20\%, 35\%, 50\%, 60\%, 70\%, 80\% and 90\%, each rounded to the nearest layer. The move is added once, at the last prompt position, while the model reads the prompt; generation then proceeds without changes. The third answer, and the other answer for right answers, are drawn at random from answers of the same kind that have a centroid. Right answers are a random sample of 200 per model. Wrong answers whose given answer has no centroid are excluded from the numbers in Section~\ref{sec:steer}.

\paragraph{Size check.} The check uses Llama-3.2-1B on a laptop and the same \SizeN{} wrong answers as the main moves (script: \texttt{wisdom/size\_check.py}). Each move is rescaled to a fixed share of the median length of test states at its layer; random moves use a fixed random direction per question and layer.

\paragraph{Near misses.} The centroid rank is computed in whitened states, for wrong answers whose right answer has a centroid; all three numbers are computed on these same wrong answers. The output rank uses the model's log-probability, at the first position of its reply, of the first token of each candidate's names, taking the best name per candidate. As a check, logistic regressions on three geometric features (distance to the right centroid, its rank, and the margin between the two nearest centroids) and on the reply's log-probability and length, scored by five-fold cross-validation with each subject kept in one fold, give AUC \AucGeoMin{}--\AucGeoMax{} against \AucConfMin{}--\AucConfMax{}.
