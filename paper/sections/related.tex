\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reading intermediate layers.}
Decoding intermediate states through the output layer, the logit lens~\citep{nostalgebraist2020interpreting}, and its trained variant, the tuned lens~\citep{belrose2023eliciting}, show predictions sharpening with depth; Future Lens~\citep{pal2023future} and Patchscopes~\citep{ghandeharioun2024patchscopes} read richer content from a single hidden state. These methods read the output vocabulary. Our places are fitted from the states themselves, and reading them does not need the answer words' output directions.

\paragraph{How depth is used.}
Several studies find that layers play different roles: early layers gather and detokenise, middle layers build abstract features, and late layers refine a prediction~\citep{lad2024the,csordas2025do,gupta2025how,cheng2024emergence,skean2025layer}. Correct and incorrect predictions follow similar layer-wise confidence curves~\citep{kim2025on}. In factual recall, relation and then subject information move to the last position, where attention heads extract the attribute~\citep{geva2023dissecting}; feed-forward layers act as key--value memories~\citep{geva2020transformer}, and middle-layer feed-forward modules at the subject's last token are causally decisive~\citep{meng2022locating}. Our jump in readability marks, for a whole population of questions, the depth at which each model's answer becomes available at the last position.

\paragraph{Shared concept spaces and representation geometry.}
Middle layers represent the same content in a shared space across languages and modalities~\citep{wendler2024do,wu2024the}. Concepts have structured geometry: categories form simplices~\citep{park2024the}, some features are circular~\citep{engels2024not}, space and time are linearly decodable~\citep{gurnee2023language}, counts lie on curved manifolds that attention reshapes~\citep{gurnee2026when}, and truth has a linear direction~\citep{marks2023the}; Goodfire's Neural Geometry series~\citep{goodfire2026the} argues that such structure is central to understanding models. In trained classifiers, same-class representations separate from other classes by a roughly constant factor per layer~\citep{he2022a}, and language models move partway toward neural collapse (states gathering at their class means) at their top layer, further with scale~\citep{wu2024linguistic}; the residual stream contains stable regions within which small changes leave the next-token prediction unchanged~\citep{janiak2024characterizing}, and yet no two prompts map to the same state~\citep{nikolaou2025language}. Places are consistent with both: questions can gather in the directions that carry the answer while remaining distinct in others.

\paragraph{What models know but do not say.}
Internal states often encode more than the output shows~\citep{orgad2024llms,gekhman2025insideout,ruscio2026on}, but on knowledge benchmarks such as MMLU and ARC the gap is near zero~\citep{park2025bridging}, and internal states mostly reflect what the model recalls rather than what is true~\citep{cheang2026do}. False few-shot demonstrations can override a correct internal answer at a critical layer~\citep{halawi2023overthinking}, and users' suggestions shift answers~\citep{sharma2023towards}. For multiple choice, the answer letter is selected by attention in a few middle layers, and a sparse set of heads then raises its probability~\citep{wiegreffe2024answer}; the winning option is decided in content space before it is bound to its letter~\citep{wong2026when}; and models carry a prior over letters~\citep{zheng2023large}.

\paragraph{Steering.}
Adding a direction to the residual stream changes behaviour~\citep{panickssery2023steering,li2023inferencetime}. Our pushes are of this kind, but each one moves a single question's state from one measured place to another, at one layer, and is compared with random pushes of the same length.
